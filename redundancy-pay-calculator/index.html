How it works

Statutory redundancy formula

Under age 22

Half a week's pay for each complete year worked while under 22.

Age 22 to 40

One week's pay for each complete year worked from age 22 up to age 40.

Age 41 and over

One and a half week's pay for each complete year worked aged 41 or over.

Eligibility reminder: Employees normally need at least two years of continuous service. Contractual redundancy schemes can pay more than the statutory minimum.
Worked example

How service years are allocated

Age 45 with 12 years of service

The calculator counts backwards from the redundancy date. Four completed years fall at age 41 or over and the remaining eight years fall in the age 22 to 40 band.

The resulting weeks of pay are multiplied by weekly pay capped at £751.

Scope: This calculator is intended for statutory redundancy pay in England, Wales and Scotland. Northern Ireland uses different statutory limits and guidance.
Common questions

Redundancy Pay FAQ

Do part years of service count?

No. Statutory redundancy pay uses completed years of continuous service.

What if I have more than 20 years of service?

Only the most recent 20 completed years count towards the statutory calculation.

Is redundancy pay taxed?

Qualifying termination payments can have special tax treatment, but notice pay, holiday pay and other elements may be taxed differently. Check the breakdown provided by the employer.

Can an employer pay more?

Yes. A contract, policy or negotiated settlement can provide an enhanced redundancy payment above the statutory minimum.

Are my details stored?

No. The calculation runs locally in your browser.
