How it works

Paint calculation method

The estimate combines wall area, optional ceiling area, coats, product coverage and your chosen extra allowance.

Wall area

2 × (length + width) × height

Door and window area is then subtracted.

Coated area

Paintable area × number of coats

The ceiling is added before coats when selected.

Paint required

Coated area ÷ coverage

The extra allowance is then applied and tins are rounded up.

Worked example

Example room estimate

4 m × 3 m room with 2.4 m walls

The wall perimeter is 14 metres, giving 33.6 m² of wall area before subtracting doors and windows. With 4 m² of openings, the base wall area becomes 29.6 m².

For two coats at 10 m² per litre, plus a 10% allowance, the calculator estimates the litres required and rounds up to the selected tin size.

Planning estimate: Actual coverage varies by brand, surface texture, colour change, preparation, application method and whether primer is required. Check the manufacturer's instructions before buying.
Common questions

Paint Calculator FAQ

How much area does one litre of paint cover?

Coverage varies by product and surface. Many interior paints advertise a coverage figure on the label, which is the best value to enter.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Subtracting their combined area gives a more precise estimate. Leaving them included can provide a useful small allowance.

Does the estimate include multiple coats?

Yes. The total paintable area is multiplied by the number of coats entered.

Should I include the ceiling?

Select the ceiling option only when you plan to paint it with the same paint and coverage assumptions.

Are my room measurements stored?

No. The calculation runs locally in your browser and does not require an account.
