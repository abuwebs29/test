How it works

Regular-hours calculation

For a full leave year, regular workers are generally entitled to 5.6 weeks of paid statutory leave, subject to a 28-day cap.

Holiday days

Days worked each week × 5.6

The result is capped at 28 days.

Holiday hours

Holiday days × typical hours per day

This provides a useful equivalent for fixed-length working days.

Bank holidays

An employer can include bank holidays as part of statutory annual leave.

Worked examples

Holiday entitlement examples

Five days per week

5 × 5.6 gives 28 days. At 7.5 hours per day, that is an equivalent of 210 holiday hours.

Three days per week

3 × 5.6 gives 16.8 days. If each working day is 8 hours, the equivalent is 134.4 hours.

General estimate only: This tool is designed for regular weekly working patterns over a full leave year. Contracts may offer more leave, and different rules apply to irregular-hours, part-year, shift, compressed-hours and part-year starters.
Common questions

Holiday Entitlement FAQ

How is statutory holiday entitlement calculated?

For someone working a regular number of days each week for a full leave year, the usual calculation is 5.6 multiplied by the number of days worked each week, capped at 28 days.

Are bank holidays included?

An employer can include bank holidays within the statutory annual leave entitlement.

Does this work for irregular-hours workers?

No. Irregular-hours and part-year workers use different accrual rules based on hours worked, so this regular-pattern calculator is not suitable for them.

Can an employer offer more holiday?

Yes. A contract can provide more leave than the statutory minimum.

Are my working details stored?

No. The calculation runs locally in your browser and does not require an account.
