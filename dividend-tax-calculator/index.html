How it works

Dividend tax calculation

1. Personal Allowance

The standard £12,570 allowance is reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000.

2. Dividend allowance

The first £500 of dividend income is taxed at 0%, but still occupies space within the tax bands.

3. Band-by-band tax

Remaining dividends are allocated across the basic, higher and additional dividend-rate bands.

Important: This is an estimate. It does not model every relief, loss, foreign dividend, trust distribution, Marriage Allowance or special tax circumstance.

Common questions

Dividend tax FAQ

Are dividends inside an ISA taxable?

No. Dividend income received inside an ISA is generally free from UK Income Tax and should not be entered here.

Does the £500 allowance reduce taxable income?

The allowance applies a 0% rate to up to £500 of dividend income. Those dividends still use space in your tax bands.

Can dividends be taxed at more than one rate?

Yes. If dividends cross a band boundary, different portions can be taxed at the basic, higher and additional dividend rates.

Are Scottish dividend rates different?

No. The UK dividend rates apply across the UK, although Scottish non-dividend income bands can affect where dividend income begins. This simplified calculator uses the UK-wide basic-rate limit for dividend stacking.
