How it works

Date calculation assumptions

This tool adds the notice length you enter to the notice date. It does not decide which notice period legally applies.

Calendar days

The selected number of days is added directly to the notice date, including weekends and bank holidays.

Weeks

Each week is treated as seven calendar days.

Months

Calendar months are added to the notice date. Month-end dates may roll to the closest valid calendar result.

Worked examples

Notice period examples

Four weeks' notice

If notice is given on 1 August and the notice period is four weeks, the calculator adds 28 calendar days to estimate the final day.

One calendar month

If notice is given on 15 September and the notice period is one month, the calculator adds one calendar month to estimate the final day.

Date estimate only: Contracts, collective agreements, statutory minimums, dismissal rules, garden leave, payment in lieu of notice, an agreed early release and the wording of when notice starts can all affect the actual final working day.
Common questions

Notice Period Calculator FAQ

Does this determine my legal notice period?

No. The tool calculates a date from the length you enter. Your employment contract and applicable statutory rules determine the correct notice period.

Are weekends and bank holidays excluded?

No. Calendar days and weeks include weekends and bank holidays. Check whether your contract uses different wording.

Can garden leave change my last day at work?

Yes. Garden leave may mean employment continues while you do not attend work, so your last active working day and employment end date can differ.

What is payment in lieu of notice?

Payment in lieu may allow employment to end without working the notice period, subject to the contract and applicable employment rules.

Are my dates stored?

No. The calculation runs locally in your browser and does not require an account.
