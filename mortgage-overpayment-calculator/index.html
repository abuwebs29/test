How it works

Why overpayments can save money

Mortgage interest is charged on the outstanding balance, so reducing that balance sooner can lower future interest.

Normal payment

The calculator first works out the standard monthly repayment for the remaining balance, rate and term.

Extra principal

Your monthly overpayment is added to the normal payment and applied against the outstanding balance.

Estimated savings

The revised repayment schedule is compared with the original schedule to estimate interest and time saved.

Worked example

Example overpayment plan

£240,000 balance with a £200 monthly overpayment

With 25 years remaining at 4.5%, the calculator first estimates the normal monthly repayment. It then adds £200 each month and recalculates the balance until the mortgage is repaid.

The difference between the original and revised schedules is shown as estimated interest saved and time saved.

Check your mortgage terms: Some lenders limit penalty-free overpayments or charge early repayment fees. This estimate assumes the interest rate remains unchanged and the overpayment is made every month.
Common questions

Mortgage Overpayment FAQ

Does this support lump-sum overpayments?

No. This version models a regular monthly overpayment. A separate lump-sum option can be added later.

Will my lender reduce the term automatically?

Not always. Some lenders reduce future monthly payments instead. Ask your lender how overpayments are applied.

Can the result be exact?

No. Actual results depend on daily interest, payment dates, rate changes, fees and lender rounding.

What happens if I enter no overpayment?

The revised schedule matches the normal repayment schedule, so estimated time and interest saved will be zero.

Are my mortgage details stored?

No. The calculation runs locally in your browser and does not require an account.
