How it works

Charge calculation

Child Benefit

The first child is calculated at £27.05 per week. Each additional child is calculated at £17.90 per week.

Taper

The charge is 1% of Child Benefit for every £200 of adjusted net income above £60,000.

Full repayment

At £80,000 or more, the charge normally equals 100% of the Child Benefit received.

Important: This is an estimate. The person with the higher adjusted net income is generally responsible for the charge. Relationship changes, partial-year claims, opt-outs and unusual income adjustments can affect the official result.
Common questions

High Income Child Benefit Charge FAQ

What counts as adjusted net income?

It broadly starts with total taxable income, including savings interest, dividends and taxable employment benefits, before Personal Allowance, then deducts certain reliefs such as qualifying pension contributions and Gift Aid.

Does household income get combined?

No. The test is based on the individual adjusted net income of the higher earner, not combined household income.

Can the charge be paid through PAYE?

For qualifying taxpayers from the 2025/26 tax year onwards, HMRC may allow the charge to be collected through a tax code. Otherwise it may need to be paid through Self Assessment.

Should someone still claim Child Benefit if they opt out of payments?

Claiming can protect National Insurance credits for a non-working parent or carer and can help ensure the child receives a National Insurance number automatically. Check current HMRC guidance for your circumstances.
